# Compliance and opt-outs

Get this wrong and you are not just annoying people – you are exposing the campaign. The mechanics are handled for you; the judgement is not.

> This page describes how the platform behaves and the habits that keep a program safe. It is not legal advice. Obligations differ by jurisdiction and by what kind of organisation you are – check yours.

## What the platform does for you

- **An automated opt-out compliance check runs as part of the send**, so problems surface before you commit rather than after.
- **An opt-out ledger keeps the record.** Anyone who opts out is automatically excluded from every future send, across the whole account, permanently.

That last point matters more than it sounds. The exclusion is account-wide, not per-list. Someone who opts out of your fundraising program does not reappear in your volunteer recruitment program because a different organiser built a different segment.

You do not maintain a suppression list by hand, and you are not relying on a volunteer remembering.

## What is still your job

**Consent.** The ledger records who left. It cannot tell you whether the people on your list ever agreed to hear from you in the first place. That is a question about how the list was built, and it is the one that actually creates risk.

Keep a source column on every record – petition, event, purchased list, previous campaign – so you can answer "why do we have this person's number" without guessing. See [Audiences and segments](/docs/audiences-and-segments).

**Identifying yourself.** Recipients should be able to tell who is contacting them from the message itself, without replying to find out.

**Authorisation statements.** Electoral communication in Australia generally carries an authorisation. What is required depends on the jurisdiction and the type of communication – confirm the current rules with your electoral commission each cycle, because they change.

**Honouring the spirit, not just the word.** Someone who replies "please stop", "unsubscribe", "no thanks" or an unambiguous expletive has opted out, whatever the exact keyword was. Watch the shared inbox for these and record them.

## Cadence is a compliance issue, not just a courtesy

Most complaints are not about the first message. They are about the fourth in a fortnight. Volume produces opt-outs, opt-outs shrink your list permanently, and a shrinking list is a strategic problem as much as a legal one.

Two habits:

- **Suppress recent contacts.** Exclude anyone contacted in the last N days from the next send.
- **Watch the opt-out rate per send, not in aggregate.** A jump on one send tells you that message was wrong for that list. Change the message rather than pushing more volume through.

## Handle a complaint properly

When someone complains rather than simply opting out:

1. **Stop contacting them immediately**, across every program.
2. **Answer once, briefly, from a real person.** Say how you got their details and confirm they have been removed.
3. **Record it.** A complaint you cannot find later is a complaint you cannot explain later.
4. **Look for the pattern.** One complaint is noise. Five on the same send is a message or a list problem.

## Before every send, the short check

- Is this list one we can justify contacting?
- Does the message identify us?
- Does it carry the authorisation it needs?
- Have we excluded recent contacts?
- Is someone rostered to read the replies?

Five questions, thirty seconds, and it catches nearly everything.
